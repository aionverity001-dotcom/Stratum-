# Interactive website direction: working brief

The website grows from an accumulated life rather than a conventional portfolio. It is a scrapbook wall, associative mind map, and choose-your-path story space.

## Material
Artifacts may include concert tickets, flyers, photographs, notes, news clippings, book passages, writing, novels, research, AI conversations, experiments, travel, people, places, and remembered events. An artifact retains its own identity. Connections and context attach to it.

## Navigation
People move by recognition, association, recurrence, and relationship, as well as by time. A ticket may lead to a venue, band, person, night, later memory, or a different interpretation years afterward. Similar situations may connect across different periods without pretending they are the same event.

Some items open a small note. Others open a collection, map, room, portal, or branching story. Visitors can wander and choose their own route. The same material may mean different things to different visitors.

## Visual language
80s/90s punk, grunge, photocopied zines, hand-made collage, record-store walls, concert posters, stickers, ticket stubs, graffiti, and found objects. Black and white form the base; color appears sparingly as an interaction cue or occasional mark. The result should feel lived-in, layered, irreverent, warm, and navigable.

## Interaction principle
The system should preserve the source artifact and its context while allowing multiple paths through it. A visitor's route is their own experience of the material. A small artifact should be allowed to remain small; a deep branch should open only where the material supports it.

## Relationship to Stratum
Stratum is the likely archival/accumulation layer. The interactive website is the visitor-facing experience built from that archive. This is a working relationship between the two project layers, not a final technical architecture.
